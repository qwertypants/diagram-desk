A restart must preserve an approval decision without repeating an uncertain external action. Durable state and idempotency solve different parts of that problem.

## The problem

A long-running job waits for approval and may crash while an external operation is in flight.

## Read the architecture

Follow the persisted approval through execution, unknown outcome, reconciliation, and terminal state.

### Walk through it

#### 1. Await approval

Persist the requested operation and approval state. A restart restores the existing decision. A rejection or expired deadline ends the job.

#### 2. Execute and recover

Run the approved operation with an idempotency key. A known transient failure can back off within a deadline; an unknown outcome must be reconciled first.

#### 3. Check the result

A verified receipt reaches completion. Exhausted retry budgets and invalid outcomes become explicit intervention states.

## Design decisions

- Persist approval and external-operation identity before execution.
- Retry activities that can safely repeat; do not infer exactly-once execution from a durable workflow.

## Failure modes

- A crash between applying an effect and recording its receipt creates uncertainty.
- Permanent errors need correction rather than repeated retries.

## Key takeaways

- Durability preserves state; idempotency controls repeated effects.
