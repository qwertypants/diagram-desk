A refund is a real side effect. The assistant may propose it, but approval, execution, and verification need separate owners.

## The problem

A support assistant may read an order, but issuing a refund needs scoped human permission.

## Read the architecture

Find the authorization gate before the refund and the receipt or reconciliation path after it.

### Walk through it

#### 1. Propose and authorize

Load order context. Treat the model’s proposed tool call as data. Check the operation, arguments, caller scope, and budget in application code.

#### 2. Approve and execute

Read-only permitted work can proceed. A refund requires approval for that specific order and amount. Denied or forbidden requests stop.

#### 3. Verify or reconcile

Execute the refund with an idempotency key. Check the receipt. After a timeout, look up the original operation before attempting another effect.

## Design decisions

- Bind approval to the exact operation and arguments.
- Use an external receipt to substantiate success.

## Failure modes

- Prompt injection can propose an unauthorized tool call.
- An expired approval must not authorize changed arguments.
- An uncertain refund must not be retried with a fresh key.

## Key takeaways

- The model suggests; the application authorizes and executes.
- Budgets and terminal states bound the loop.
