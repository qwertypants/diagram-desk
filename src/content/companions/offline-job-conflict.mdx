In this illustrated field-service policy, an offline completion can be accepted even when the assignment changed. The server considers what changed in each field instead of selecting one entire record as the winner.

Maya repairs a heater while her tablet is offline. The dispatcher, unaware of that progress, moves the job from Truck A to Truck B. Maya's completion and the new assignment can both describe real actions.

<DiagramEmbed slug="offline-job-conflict" title="When a Job Completes While It Moves" heading="Separate the two changes" cue="Follow Complete offline and Move A to B independently. Then trace Field-level check to accepted status, retained assignment, and the review that closes B." companion={true} />

The tablet saves completion locally and queues a durable action. Reconnecting pulls server events after a cursor, the saved position in that history, then sends the action with its stable identity and base version. A wake-up message only prompts catch-up; it does not replace the event history.

At **Field-level check**, **Status unchanged** allows **Accept completion**, while **assignment changed** leads to **Retain assignment**. The completion is audited, and the disagreement creates a review item. **Close B assignment** ends the unnecessary dispatch while **Completion stands** preserves Maya's work and note.

This is a chosen domain rule, not a general merge algorithm. If the job was cancelled or already completed, the unchanged-status condition would not hold and another policy would be needed. Version checking exposes the conflict; it cannot decide what the business should accept.
