After a crash, resume the recorded workflow state. Do not treat an existing approval or an uncertain external action as a new request.

Imagine a manager approving a paid translation order. The application must remember which document, languages, and quoted cost were approved, even if it restarts hours later. It also needs a stable identity for the provider operation.

<DiagramEmbed slug="durable-recovery" title="An approval that survives a crash" heading="Read the recovery branches separately" cue="Follow Awaiting approval to Executing, then compare retryable failure with unknown outcome. Completed requires a verified Check outcome." companion={true} />

A known **retryable failure** enters **Backoff** and can return to execution within its deadline. An **unknown outcome** enters **Reconcile receipt** because the provider may have accepted the order before its reply disappeared.

A found receipt goes to **Check outcome**. Confirmation that nothing was applied permits a retry through backoff. Continued uncertainty needs its own waiting or intervention policy, which the chart does not fully specify.

**Denied**, **Expired**, and **Needs intervention** describe different endings. None should appear to the user as successful completion. **Completed** follows only after the receipt matches the intended operation.

Durability preserves progress. Idempotency means that retrying the same operation does not repeat its effect, and reconciliation establishes what happened externally. These responsibilities remain separate even when a workflow framework manages the recorded state.
