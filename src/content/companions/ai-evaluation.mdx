Judge an AI change against explicit tasks and expectations. Logs reveal what the system did; evaluation asks whether that behavior meets the user's needs.

Suppose a documentation assistant adopts a shorter-answer prompt. A useful test asks whether a migration answer still includes every required action, rather than rewarding brevity alone. Keep the question, source documents, and rubric stable when comparing the old and new prompts.

Follow **Traffic** through **Response** to **Traces**, then move into the evaluation paths below. **Golden cases** and **Sample cases** are two inputs to the same quality investigation.

**Rules** handle properties with exact checks. A **Model judge** can apply a source-based rubric, but needs calibration against **Human review**. A fluent answer can still omit the restart step a user needs.

Compare those findings in **Results** and the **Dashboard**, including task categories whose failures an average could hide. **Regression?** sends a failing candidate back through revision and evaluation. Its **No** branch reaches **Deploy**, but deployment also needs absolute acceptance criteria. Matching an inadequate baseline is not enough.

The chart presents a feedback structure, not a guarantee of quality. Review reference expectations when the product changes, protect sensitive data in sampled traces, and repeat important cases where model variation affects the decision.
