Researchers trained ESM-2 protein-language models with viral sequences removed, compared their benchmark performance with unfiltered models, then tested whether fine-tuning on the removed sequences restored it.

<DiagramEmbed slug="protein-data-filtering" title="What pretraining filters do and do not remove" heading="The filter changes the training corpus" cue="Start with the nested viral-data filters, follow the model training into held-out tests, then trace the lower recovery lane back to evaluation." companion={true} />

The filtered models lost some measured performance on viral-protein tasks while keeping most non-viral performance in the reported controls. Fine-tuning on removed sequences restored benchmark performance, so data filtering alone did not make that capability impossible to recover.

The study covers one model family, one seed per size, and computational benchmarks. It does not measure real-world biological outcomes. [Read the full lesson](../../articles/protein-data-filtering/) for its methods, caveats, and sources.
